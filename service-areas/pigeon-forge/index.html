Marketing for Pigeon Forge businesses

Pigeon Forge is a city of about 6,000 people that gives directions by traffic light. Ask where a show or a pancake house is and you'll hear "between light 3 and light 4," because everything here lines up along five-plus miles of Parkway. That's the whole marketing problem in one picture: dozens of businesses on one road, competing for the same visitor in the same weekend, and most of those visitors decided where they're going before they turned off I-40.

Pigeon Forge runs on a calendar

Most towns have a busy season. Pigeon Forge has a schedule. Dollywood's openings and festivals, the spring and fall Rod Runs, Winterfest lights from November into February, holiday weekends: each one changes who's in town and what they're searching for. So a flat monthly ad budget is the most common money-waster we'd expect to find here. We plan spend around the calendar instead, heavier in the weeks when people are booking and lighter when they're already sitting in Parkway traffic.

Win the night-before search

A family in a cabin up on the ridge picks tomorrow's dinner show, mini golf course and breakfast spot on a phone after the kids are asleep. They type things like "dinner show pigeon forge with kids" or "breakfast near the island," and more of them ask ChatGPT the same question. The businesses that win that moment have a fast site that answers the exact question, current hours, real photos, and a stack of recent Google reviews. That's buildable, and it's where we'd start with most Parkway businesses.

  • Pages that answer the questions visitors type, written for your show, shop or restaurant
  • Google Business Profile cleanup, so hours and photos match what people find when they arrive
  • AEO work so AI trip planners can name you, not just the big ticket sites
  • Ad budgets timed to the event calendar instead of spread evenly across the year

Direct bookings for cabins and cottages

Rental owners here pay the booking platforms a cut of every stay. You can't skip the platforms entirely, and we won't tell you to. What you can do is own the second booking: collect the guest's email, follow up after checkout, and send them to a site of your own that takes the reservation. We built Downtown River Rentals, riverside cottages a short walk off the Parkway, around exactly that job. The CRM side is what makes it work, because a returning guest you never contact again is just a platform customer.

When the phone rings during a rush

On a Saturday in October, nobody at a busy attraction or rental office is free to answer every call. Missed-call text-back and an AI assistant that answers the routine questions (parking, check-in time, pets) keep those callers from dialing the next listing. Kodak is close enough that we'll come set it up in person and train whoever answers your phones.

What Pigeon Forge owners ask us

Rod Run weekends bring the Parkway to a standstill. Should my ads run then?

Usually less than you'd think. During the spring and fall Rod Runs, the people already in town are stuck in traffic, and your cheapest customers are the ones who searched you before they left home. We push budget into the weeks before an event and trim it during, unless you sell something people buy on the spot.

Can a vacation rental in Pigeon Forge really get direct bookings?

Yes, from repeat guests and from searches the big platforms answer badly. A guest who stayed once and liked it is the easiest booking you'll ever get, if you have their email and a site that takes a reservation without the platform fee. That's exactly the job we built the Downtown River Rentals site to do.

My show or attraction sells tickets online. What does a marketing agency add?

Mostly the stretch before the purchase. Families compare three or four shows from the cabin the night before, so we build pages that answer what they're actually asking (run time, whether a toddler will sit through it, what dinner includes) and track which ads and searches end in a ticket sale.

Do you work with Pigeon Forge businesses that aren't tourist-facing?

We do. Plenty of Pigeon Forge businesses mostly serve the people who live here and the cabin owners: cleaners, maintenance crews, property managers, contractors. For them it's local search and fast follow-up, and a CRM that keeps owner leads from getting buried under guest calls.
